# Business, monetization, and launch audit

Date: 2026-11-06. Scope: repository inspection and public provider pages; no customer interviews, production telemetry, financial records, live service validation, or legal review were provided. All targets, prices, conversion rates, and costs below are hypotheses, not observed results. Public prices can change; recheck checkout and terms before launch.

## Recommendation

Start with individual software-engineering job seekers who have an actual upcoming interview and a job description. Sell a short, role-specific practice loop: upload a resume and job description, practice aloud or in text, receive actionable feedback, and rehearse again. Position it as preparation, not live assistance in a real interview or a promise of hiring outcomes. The strongest initial test is whether people complete a second relevant mock and pay for a bounded interview-preparation pack; a sustained subscription is a later hypothesis because job searches are episodic.

## Customer and market hypothesis

**Initial ICP:** English-speaking early-career and mid-level software engineers with a scheduled interview within 30 days, a resume and a specific job description, especially candidates without easy access to a practice partner. Buyer and user are the same person. Narrow initial distribution to developer job-search communities, alumni groups, and career coaches with permission to share; interview at least 15 qualified candidates before extrapolating. Screen out casual browsing when testing willingness to pay. Test adjacent product/analyst roles only after the software-engineer loop works.

**Job to be done:** “Before my next interview, help me rehearse realistic questions about my experience and this role, then identify the one or two changes worth practicing.” Differentiation to test: specific references to the user's supplied role and resume; actionable, non-fabricated feedback; a fast repeat practice loop. These are hypotheses, not demonstrated advantages. Do not claim better hiring outcomes without controlled evidence.

**Competitive context, checked 2026-11-06:** [Aced (formerly Exponent)](https://www.tryexponent.com/upgrade) lists a free plan with five peer mocks/month and an AI tier with AI mocks and feedback; its page shows multiple monthly/annual prices in the same text, so verify the selected billing period at checkout. [interviewing.io's FAQ](https://interviewing.io/faq) says human expert mock interviews start at $179, varying by subject and interviewer. This is a premium human-service comparator, not an equivalent AI product. [Final Round AI](https://docs.finalroundai.com/docs/getting-started/subscribe-and-plans) describes AI debriefs and real-time interview assistance; its checkout displays plan-specific prices, so no price is asserted here. These examples establish alternatives, not market size or willingness to pay for this product.

## Pricing and monetization experiments

Proposed prices are deliberately bounded and unvalidated; do not claim the product currently sells them.

1. **Free activation:** one complete tailored mock plus actionable report, with a clearly disclosed allowance and no card. Enforce cost ceilings server-side, including retries and abandoned sessions; never truncate the promised report after a user has done the work.
2. **Primary test:** $19 one-time pack for five additional completed mocks usable over 90 days, with a reasonable session length, disclosed expiration and support/refund policy. Compare to a $29 pack in a randomized, consistently exposed offer test; use the same entitlement. Small samples are directional, not proof. Track paid conversion, completion, refunds, usage and cost by assigned cohort; do not change price mid-checkout.
3. **Secondary test:** $19/month for up to ten mocks per billing month, cancelable before renewal, only after recurring retention is observable. Compare net revenue and contribution per acquired user against the pack, not conversion alone. Avoid “unlimited” until tail usage and abuse are understood; current pro code is unlimited, so implement enforceable entitlements first.
4. **BYOK:** treat as a separate experiment, not a substitute for billing or a security control. The existing exemption bypasses action metering; assess key storage, scope, abuse and residual infrastructure cost. Do not imply that the provider is the only cost.

Instrument offer exposure, checkout started, payment succeeded, entitlement granted, use, refunds, failed/reversed payments and cancellation. Grant access only from authenticated payment-provider events with idempotency and reconciliation; enforce limits on the server, make checkout/refund terms explicit, and test failure paths before taking money. No payment integration was established in this inspection.

## Unit economics: formulas and illustrative scenarios

Measure actual provider/model usage, document processing, storage, retries, support time and payment fees per completed session and per cohort. `Session variable cost = model input + model output + speech/transcription (if used) + embeddings/document processing + attributable infrastructure + retry/failure cost + variable support`. `Contribution per purchase = net receipts after refunds and fees − all session and non-session variable costs`; `contribution margin = contribution / gross receipts`. `CAC = attributable acquisition spend / new paying customers`; `payback = CAC / monthly contribution` only when a recurring cohort has demonstrated a meaningful contribution stream. Account for free-user cost in cohort economics: `contribution per acquired signup = paid conversion × contribution per payer − free cost per signup − acquisition cost per signup`. Do not use unobserved lifetime or assume constant churn to manufacture LTV.

Illustrations, USD, entirely assumed, excluding tax and fixed salaries: $19 pack, $1 payment/refund allowance, five completed sessions, and $0.50 other variable costs per buyer. Low/base/high session cost of $0.20/$0.60/$1.50 yields contribution of $16.50/$14.50/$10.00 and gross-receipt contribution margins of 86.8%/76.3%/52.6%, respectively. At the base case, a 10% signup-to-paid conversion and $0.30 free cost per signup yields $1.15 contribution per signup before acquisition. At $1 acquisition per signup the contribution is $0.15; at $2 it is −$0.85. These are sensitivity exercises, not forecasts; unused credits, mixed usage, refunds, taxes, and fixed overhead change the result.

As an external reference only, [OpenAI's API pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing) lists `gpt-live-transcribe` at $0.017 per minute and `gpt-transcribe` at $0.0045 per minute as checked. A hypothetical 20-minute transcription would therefore cost $0.34 or $0.09 respectively before all other costs. This repository's actual provider/model and audio pathway must be measured; these are not estimates of its current costs. Provider rates, token volume, retries and silent time can materially change economics.

## Beta funnel and decision rules

Recruit 30–50 qualified opt-in users over 2–4 weeks through direct outreach and relevant communities; use consented feedback interviews, not unsolicited bulk messaging. Suggested instrumentation uses random user/session identifiers, timestamps, state and cost aggregates rather than resumes, transcripts or answer text in analytics. Define events once and count distinct users at each step:

| Stage | Event and denominator | Initial decision target (hypothesis) |
|---|---|---|
| Acquisition | qualified landing visitors → signup | Record channel and interview date; no target until baseline. |
| Setup | signups → valid resume and job description submitted within 24h | ≥60%. Measure upload and link-extraction failures. |
| Activation | signups → first mock completed and report delivered within 24h | ≥40%; report delivery must be included, not just session start. |
| Value | first-report recipients → rate feedback useful or very useful | ≥70% among respondents; publish response rate and review nonresponse bias. |
| Repeat | activated users → second completed mock within 7 days | ≥30%, alongside qualitative evidence that the feedback drove a change. |
| Payment | eligible activated users → paid pack purchase within 7 days after exposure | ≥10% directional; report denominators, cohort size, refund rates and price arm. |
| Reliability | started sessions → completed or user-cancelled; report delivery | ≥95% technical success excluding explicit user cancellation; record and investigate failures separately. |

These thresholds are gates for learning, not statistical claims. Review weekly, segment by source, job proximity, input mode and role, and record interview findings and reasons for abandonment. With small samples, quote counts and uncertainty; run more users before a hard pricing conclusion. Personally interview users who fail, succeed and decline to pay. Track seven-day repeat and outcome feedback without claiming the product caused interviews or offers.

## Launch stages and gates

1. **Internal rehearsal:** execute real end-to-end accounts, document upload, role input, session, report, return, delete and failure/retry flows; verify access control, quota behavior, customer-data handling, browser compatibility, cost tracking, support owner and recovery. Use test data; check provider and infrastructure configuration. Establish a runbook and feedback-quality review by people familiar with interviews.
2. **Private beta:** invite the qualified cohort gradually, cap total volume and cost, provide an explicit support channel, monitor the funnel and review feedback for inaccurate or invented claims. Keep claims narrow; capture defects, latency and cost. Pause recruitment if data isolation, persistent report failures or runaway cost are found.
3. **Paid pilot:** only after reliable delivery and payment/entitlement, cancellation/refund, support and incident workflows are tested. Offer bounded packs to a subset, review each dispute, and validate contribution after refunds and support. Do not describe current quota code as billing.
4. **Public launch:** require repeat usage and evidence of willingness to pay in more than one cohort, budget and alerting for tail usage, reliable onboarding, accessible privacy/terms and deletion procedures, production monitoring and an incident owner. Expand channels one at a time and compare paid acquisition against actual cohort contribution. Retain a free user path with a hard budget.

## B2B expansion

**Second-stage ICP:** university career centers or small coding bootcamps supporting cohorts of software-engineering job seekers. The economic buyer may be a career-services director, while students are the users; coaches need aggregate engagement and opt-in support, not automatic access to private transcripts. Validate with 10 buyer interviews and 2–3 time-limited pilots before building enterprise features. Ask about procurement, cohort size, incumbent tools, consent, renewal calendar, measurable outcomes and who controls student data.

**Pilot proposal, not existing pricing:** 8 weeks, up to 50 invited students, a capped allowance of five mocks each, onboarding and aggregated weekly usage, for a proposed $1,000 fixed pilot. This is $20 per invited seat at full enrollment and at most 250 mocks; at the illustrative base $0.60 per mock, model-session variable costs alone can reach $150, before onboarding, support, infrastructure, payments and unused seats. Negotiate scope and renewal criteria; charge only after operational and privacy gates are met. Compare paid institutional pilots against free alternatives and the time of a human coach. Never infer a scalable margin from a single pilot.

Pilot success: an agreed enrollment denominator, ≥50% activation among invited students, ≥30% second-mock rate among activated students, feedback quality assessed by users/coaches, a positive buyer review and written renewal intent. These are proposed targets; record actual counts and reasons for non-use. Review student consent, accessibility, retention/deletion, provider subprocessors, contractual confidentiality and permitted reporting with the buyer before data is shared. Any customer-facing report should avoid individual-level content without explicit permission.

**Expansion gates:** demonstrate repeatable student activation and at least two independently renewed paid pilots; then design organization membership and tenant isolation, admin provisioning, aggregate reporting, documented retention and deletion, billing and procurement documentation. SSO, contractual service levels, security attestations and integrations should be driven by specific buyer requirements and validated capability, not promised in advance. Keep the product in candidate preparation; employment assessment and selection introduce materially different validity, bias, privacy and legal requirements and should be treated as a separate product decision with qualified review.

## Principal risks and responses

- **Feedback harms or invents claims:** evaluate groundedness and usefulness with human reviewers, show the evidence behind suggestions, allow correction and collect user reports; no hiring guarantee.
- **Sensitive resumes, job descriptions and interview answers:** minimize collection and telemetry, verify isolation and deletion across stores and vendors, disclose processing and retention, and investigate incidents promptly. Code presence is not compliance certification.
- **Unpredictable cost or abuse:** meter action and actual model/provider usage, limit session length and concurrency, budget free traffic, inspect BYOK exemption and set spend alerts. Pro unlimited is a material launch risk.
- **Poor retention and episodic intent:** prefer packs while measuring repeat cohorts; do not depend on subscription lifetime assumptions.
- **Competitive pressure and distribution:** validate the role-specific feedback loop and acquisition channel before scaling ad spend; competitors have free offerings and strong established content.
- **B2B privacy and trust:** do not expose student content by default; verify multi-tenancy and procurement requirements before presenting an institutional product as ready.
