## Current position and outcome

The repository contains authenticated interview preparation, resume analysis, role targeting, reports and usage controls. Code presence does not establish operational readiness or effectiveness. The landing redesign is implemented locally: production build, 10 Chromium checks (including signup failure/retry) and 46 frontend unit tests pass. A browser preview verified the desktop headline and a 375 px mobile viewport without horizontal overflow. Full accessibility, real-service integration and deployed verification remain open; see [verification](verification.md). The beta signup endpoint passes its focused tests as part of 26 backend checks using real Python libraries and mocked Firebase. Atomic storage calls are tested; deployed Firestore persistence and operational readiness remain unverified. The three audits are snapshots; recheck each finding against the final revision and deployed configuration.

Target outcome: a candidate with a specific upcoming software-engineering interview supplies a resume and job description, completes a relevant practice interview, receives grounded actionable feedback, and can return for a second practice session. The initial business is individual preparation, not hiring assessment or assistance in a real employer interview. Do not promise voice capability until the actual end-to-end pathway is verified.

## Working conventions

Owners are suggested accountable roles, not assigned individuals. Estimates are rough engineer-days (ED), excluding procurement, legal review and external dependencies; they are not delivery commitments. Design, product and operations work needs its own capacity. Tasks may overlap with adequate staffing, but a phase gate cannot pass on a code change alone. An owner records commit and deployed revision, environment, test results, outstanding risks and sign-off for each gate. Security findings described as conditional on deployment require inspection of deployed Firestore rules, IAM and network controls; static review is not evidence of an incident.

## Product experience and messaging

Keep first use focused on one role and one practice session. Explain what inputs are required, how documents are stored and reused, and what feedback can and cannot establish. Make retry, support and deletion paths visible; use a manual job-description fallback when a link fails. Treat reports as coaching suggestions, never as verified employment predictions. Resume tailoring is a future feature until the disabled action has a working, reviewable, reversible flow. Test content with users rather than presenting generated scores as calibrated evidence.

For the public landing page, verify the existing sign-in callback and onboarding behavior against the final design, and update tests to assert actual accessible content and functioning entry points. The local build passing is useful but does not verify authenticated journeys, browser accessibility, or production deployment.

## Business model and experiment design

**ICP hypothesis:** English-speaking early- to mid-career software engineers with a scheduled interview within 30 days, a resume and a job description. Recruit through relevant communities, alumni networks and consenting coaches. Interview at least 15 qualified candidates and include unsuccessful users and nonbuyers. Initial differentiation to validate: role-grounded questions, concrete feedback linked to supplied evidence, and fast repeat practice. No hiring-outcome claim is established.

**Competitive reference, checked 2026-11-06:** [Aced](https://www.tryexponent.com/upgrade) lists a free plan with five peer mocks per month and an AI tier; its billing-period prices must be checked at checkout. [interviewing.io](https://interviewing.io/faq) says expert human mock interviews start at $179; human coaching is not directly equivalent to AI. [Final Round AI's plan documentation](https://docs.finalroundai.com/docs/getting-started/subscribe-and-plans) describes AI debriefs and live assistance, with prices shown in checkout. These are context, not evidence of our willingness to pay or market size.

**Proposed, not implemented prices:** offer one complete tailored mock and report free, subject to clear limits. Randomly test $19 versus $29 for five additional completed mocks over 90 days, with the same entitlement and disclosed refund and expiration terms. Consider $19/month for up to ten mocks only after measuring repeat cohorts; compare contribution per acquired user, not conversion alone. Existing `pro` is unlimited and BYOK may bypass quotas; neither is a safe commercial entitlement without the security and metering work above. Avoid unlimited promises while tail cost is unknown.

**Cost model:** session variable cost = model input + output + speech/transcription if used + document processing + allocated infrastructure + retries/failures + variable support. Contribution per purchase = receipts after fees and refunds − session and other variable cost. Contribution per acquired signup = paid conversion × contribution per payer − free cost per signup − acquisition cost per signup. CAC = attributable acquisition spend ÷ new paying customers. Use payback only for a demonstrated recurring contribution stream; do not invent lifetime value from assumed retention.

Illustrative only: a $19 five-mock pack, $1 fee/refund allowance, $0.50 other variable cost and five used sessions produces contributions of $16.50, $14.50 and $10.00 at $0.20, $0.60 and $1.50 per session respectively. At 10% conversion and $0.30 free cost per signup, the middle case yields $1.15 per signup before acquisition; a $2 acquisition cost makes it −$0.85. Actual model, support, retries, taxes, refunds and unused credits must be measured. [OpenAI pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing) lists live transcription at $0.017/min and `gpt-transcribe` at $0.0045/min as checked; these are only external rate references and are not the cost of this repository's actual audio pathway.

## Funnel and release gates

For a 30–50-person, 2–4-week qualified private beta, instrument distinct-user events for signup, valid input, started session, completed report, feedback rating, repeat mock, offer exposure, checkout, payment, refunds and cancellation. Capture timestamps, state and aggregate cost, not raw resumes or transcripts. The following are proposed learning thresholds, not forecasts or statistically significant proof:

| Metric | Denominator | Directional target |
|---|---|---:|
| Valid inputs within 24 hours | Signups | ≥60% |
| Completed mock and delivered report within 24 hours | Signups | ≥40% |
| Useful/very useful feedback | Respondents receiving reports; disclose response rate | ≥70% |
| Second completed mock within seven days | Activated users | ≥30% |
| Pack purchase within seven days of offer | Eligible activated users exposed | ≥10% |
| Technical session success | Started sessions; record explicit user cancellations separately | ≥95% |

Review counts, denominators and funnel failures weekly; small samples are directional and require further cohorts. Record provider cost, support time, refund rate and acquisition cost before scaling. Assess groundedness through human review and prompt/versioned evaluation; do not treat a high numeric score as evidence of hiring outcomes.

**Internal gate:** two-user authorization, rules, BYOK, upload and SSRF tests; privacy lifecycle; working complete practice/report; basic accessibility and failure recovery; runbooks, capped spend and monitored deployment. Record deployed rules, IAM and revision. **Private beta gate:** internal gate plus verified beta collection, consent, support owner, explicit incident response, and capped cohort. Pause recruitment for data isolation failure, recurring lost reports or uncontrolled costs. **Paid pilot gate:** private beta reliability and acceptable feedback plus tested payments, entitlements, reconciliation, refunds, cost ledger and support. **Public expansion gate:** evidence of repeat value and willingness to pay across cohorts, predictable contribution, privacy and deletion workflows, operational monitoring, rollback and sufficient capacity. No gate is complete until its evidence and responsible sign-off are recorded.

## B2B path

After individual repeat value, interview university career-center directors and coding-bootcamp operators. Validate budget holder, cohort size, current alternatives, procurement timing, accessibility, consent, privacy, support and desired aggregate outcomes. Proposed pilot: $1,000 for eight weeks, up to 50 invited students and five mocks each, with onboarding and consented aggregate weekly usage; price is a hypothesis, not an offered or implemented contract. At the illustrative $0.60 session cost, 250 mocks cost $150 before onboarding, infrastructure and support.

Agree in advance on denominators and outcomes: proposed ≥50% activation among invited students, ≥30% second-mock rate among activated users, user/coaching quality feedback, and buyer renewal intent. Avoid exposing individual transcripts without explicit permission. Require two independently renewed paid pilots and a security design before broad enterprise investment; add organization isolation, provisioning, reporting, deletion and procurement controls to satisfy actual requirements. Employer selection or assessment is a distinct product decision with different validity, fairness, privacy and legal review requirements.
